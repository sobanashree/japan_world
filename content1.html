04. Japanese Culture

Japanese culture is a rich blend of ancient heritage and modern expression. Rooted in harmony with nature, respect for community, and attention to detail, it shapes traditional arts, seasonal celebrations, daily manners, and language.

Traditional Clothing

Traditional Japanese attire reflects seasonal beauty and craftsmanship. The Kimono is a classic robe tied with a wide sash called an obi, worn for weddings, tea ceremonies, and formal events. The Yukata is a lighter cotton robe worn during summer festivals and hot spring stays, often paired with zori sandals or wooden geta.

Festivals

Japanese festivals, known as Matsuri, celebrate seasons, local shrines, harvests, and community spirit. Highlights include Hanami (cherry blossom viewing), Kyoto's Gion Matsuri floats, Obon lantern and dance traditions, and summer fireworks (hanabi taikai) with lively food stalls and drum music.

Customs and Traditions

Everyday customs in Japan emphasize politeness, cleanliness, and respect for others. Removing outdoor shoes before entering homes or traditional rooms keeps spaces clean, while bowing (ojigi) expresses greeting or gratitude. Classical arts like Chanoyu (tea ceremony) and Ikebana (flower arrangement) reflect calm focus.

Japanese Language

The Japanese language (Nihongo) uses three writing scripts together: Hiragana (curved phonetic characters for native words and grammar), Katakana (angular characters for loanwords and emphasis), and Kanji (meaning-based characters adapted from Chinese), along with polite speech levels (keigo) suited to social situations.
